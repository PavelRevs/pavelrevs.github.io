Privacy policy

Last updated: 28 October 2026

What Quaqua is

Quaqua is a private application that runs only on its owner's own computer. It has no servers of its own, and no data is sent to the Quaqua developer.

Google user data we access

When you connect a Gmail or Google Workspace mailbox, Quaqua asks for permission to read, send and organise email in that mailbox (Gmail API scopes gmail.modify and gmail.send). It uses this access only to:

  • send the emails you write and schedule in Quaqua;
  • check whether recipients replied, so follow-ups stop automatically;
  • detect bounces and opt-out requests;
  • move warm-up emails out of spam between your own mailboxes, if you turn warm-up on.

Where the data is kept

Email metadata and the text of replies to your outreach are stored in a local database on your computer. The access token Google issues is stored in the operating system's protected credential store (Windows Credential Manager), never in plain files. Nothing is uploaded to any server controlled by the developer.

Sharing

Quaqua does not sell, rent or share Google user data with anyone. Data leaves your computer only when you yourself turn on an integration (for example a notification to your own Telegram or Slack), and then only the notification you configured.

Limited use

Quaqua's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements. Google user data is not used for advertising and is not used to train AI models.

Removing access and data

You can disconnect a mailbox in Quaqua at any time, which deletes its stored token. You can also revoke access at myaccount.google.com/permissions. Deleting the Quaqua data folder removes all locally stored data.
